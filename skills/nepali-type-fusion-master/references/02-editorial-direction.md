# Editorial Direction: Premium Nepali Documentary Typography

## Index
1. Story hierarchy; 2. Authentic cinematic design; 3. Attention curve; 4. One-frame art direction; 5. Palette; 6. Human-editing grammar; 7. Reviewing concepts.

## 1. Story, not decoration

Every typography decision starts with a viewer question: **What do I need them to understand now?** Parse script into beats: hook, promise, mechanism, evidence, personal risk, solution, call to action. Mark certainty and source status for any statistics. There is no justification for a swoosh because a word merely exists.

The best explainer edit uses **contrast in density**:
- Calm statement: stable text and one restrained entrance.
- Complication: purposeful angle/change of scale or layer depth.
- Evidence: clean statistical hierarchy and clear label.
- Reveal: a single editorial accent and focal moment.
- Human cost: quieter typography, space, no cheap impact.
- Solution: positive but not flippant transition to stable call to action.

## 2. Distinct visual language

Reference modern high-retention explainer videos for clarity and narrative logic, but develop an original film identity instead of mechanically reproducing another creator's exact look. Define:
- **Type rhythm**: e.g. short assertive words / generous holds on information.
- **Camera logic**: e.g. slow 2.5D slide-left at turns; no random direction flipping.
- **Palette logic**: white=information; yellow=data or actionable terms; red=risk; desaturated cyan only when contextual and user-approved.
- **Depth logic**: background soft; text crisp; secondary layer slower; occasional foreground out-of-focus object if assets allowed.
- **Transition logic**: semantic continuity (departure arrow -> map -> destination), not preset-carousel mania.

For text-only instructions, **type is the entire composition**. Do not add ambient particles, random lines, decorative geometry, or icons as “premium.” Use whitespace and carefully timed moves.

## 3. Attention curve for a 30–60 second Reel

- Opening 0–3 s: a legible assertion or question with strong hierarchy; not five unreadable microcuts.
- 3–12 s: define stakes and visual grammar. Avoid repeating identical text entry.
- 12–40 s: alternate evidence/data with human context, use quiet cutaways and rhythm change.
- Final: actionable information, stable visible call to action, sufficient read time. A helpline must not vanish rapidly.

These are planning heuristics, not a fixed timing template. Sync final cuts to actual voice if given. NEVER invent dialogue timestamps.

## 4. Layout checkpoint method

For each critical frame create a precise plan:
- Canvas and safe areas.
- Type copy verbatim.
- Hero role and counterpart sizes.
- 3 coordinates: center of main title, baseline, margin from nearest edge.
- Ink bbox and intended optical adjustments.
- Resting state first; entrance vector second.
- An editorial explanation in one sentence.

If background is a photograph, check figure/ground separation and visual focus. Do not cover faces unless intentional and ethically appropriate. If no faces requested, do not use face-oriented footage.

## 5. Restrained palette

Example design tokens (adjust to brand):
- Background `#0E1117` near-black, alternate `#171C25`.
- White `#F4F3EF` for primary type.
- Muted light `#ADB5BE` for qualification.
- Yellow `#F4C94D` for warning data and hero number.
- Deep red `#D84B4F` for limited risk emphasis.

Color meanings are not universal; establish audience context. Check actual compositing / grade. Yellow on white, red on black at low weight, and thin white on bright scene often fail contrast. Use shadows only enough to solve readability.

## 6. Natural human-editor cadence

- Motions should have distinct **motivation**. Horizontal move signals a new idea, vertical descent implies consequence, zoom-in narrows scrutiny, camera pull-back reveals context.
- Consistent hidden anchor points make a sequence feel crafted.
- Use motivated irregularity: not every entrance precisely 0.3 s, not all from same direction.
- The strongest beat sometimes is a hard cut to quiet, not more animation.
- Have one 3D-feeling signature scene, not relentless 3D transforms.
- Avoid fake hand-crafted mistakes. Human taste means selectivity, not random jitter.

### Originality review
Ask: Could this exact sequence be made by applying one stock pack to any script? If yes, redo hero logic and transition continuity. Does the choreography change if the narration changes? If no, it may be a generic preset masquerading as story editing.

## 7. Art-director scorecard (0–5 each)

1. Script meaning immediately legible.
2. Nepali glyphs flawless.
3. Typography intentional with 1 clear hero.
4. Movement editorially justified and gracefully eased.
5. Reading time sufficient at phone size.
6. Sound tastefully supports the beat.
7. Scene differs meaningfully from adjacent scenes without breaking design grammar.
8. Project is maintainable/editable and deliverable.

Score under 32/40 requires another pass; **any** broken Nepali text or incorrect data is automatic failure regardless of overall score.
