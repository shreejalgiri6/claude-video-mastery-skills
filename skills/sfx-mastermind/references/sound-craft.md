# Cinematic Sound Craft — The Master Design Manual

This reference governs the **art** of choosing, building and directing sound. Everything here is conditional on story, visuals, truthful documentary framing, and speech clarity. Do not turn its recipes into obligatory effects.

## 1. Sound psychology: edit attention, not noise

The audience does not consciously catalogue each effect. They infer **cause, weight, distance, urgency, movement, vulnerability, and consequence**. Build those perceptions intentionally.

- **Expectation:** Silence or reduced texture just before a key fact may increase focus; a whoosh can reduce it.
- **Salience:** A sudden short spectral or transient change can direct attention, but repeated hits rapidly lose importance.
- **Contrast:** Minimalism creates room for the *one* impactful reveal. “Bigger” is often a contextual difference rather than a louder signal.
- **Causality:** The ear links an SFX to synchronized movement—even if you did not intend it. Do not imply a missing event.
- **Perceptual grouping:** Similar material, envelope and spatial treatment across a scene read as a coherent sonic language. Constantly changing stock packs sounds artificial.
- **Distance:** Distant sources generally sound quieter, less direct, with weaker high-frequency presence relative to space; exceptions depend on environment. Filter and reverberate gently rather than applying a blanket muffling preset.
- **Speed:** A quick acceleration may justify a short bright transient; a slow steady camera move typically calls for subtler evolving air/room tone, often no accent.
- **Texture:** Real-world textured detail often sells the frame more effectively than artificial sub-bass.

### Sound function vs sound object
An editorial “information reveal” is a **function**. It does not physically make noise. Choose a meaningful visual object/material cue, micro-tone, faint mechanical texture, or silence; don't automatically use synthetic UI sounds because a number moved.

## 2. The hierarchical sonic palette

Define a small “sound bible” before editing a large piece:

1. **Narrative signature:** documentary natural / restrained investigative / human intimate / sober informative / optimistic public service.
2. **Source palette:** paper, restrained mechanical click, room tone, air movement, realistic device, transit, quiet tonal pressure.
3. **Forbidden palette:** arcade game, random sci-fi glitches, obnoxious bass, trailer braaams, comic squeaks, alarm spam, generic heartbeat panic (unless explicitly motivated).
4. **Dynamics:** where to be almost empty vs active; identify 1–3 privileged hero moments.
5. **Spatial convention:** close Foley for evidence/documents, wider room presence for location, continuous air under connected shots.
6. **Transitions grammar:** e.g. gentle air-swish for 2.5D camera travel; dry micro-tick only on the biggest numerical reveal; room tone changes on real scene changes.
7. **No-sound grammar:** face/voice/intimate grief, critical helpline digits, factual correction, weighty pauses.

Design *one piece*, not a collection of disconnected stock sounds.

## 3. The four subcomponents of a premium transient

Use only needed components:

- **Attack:** the moment attention shifts. Real paper edge, snap, tiny click, short dry impact.
- **Body:** material and mass; hollow wood, dense soft thump, whispery fabric, metal resonance. If no physical cause, keep body neutral.
- **Tail:** room/reflections/sustain and smooth release; control collisions with narration and next cut.
- **Air:** motion and connection before/around the action; can carry directional animation.

A designer might layer attack from a real object with airy motion, then cut the tail short and tuck it under narration. **Every layer needs a separate audible function; remove any redundant layer.**

## 4. Timing is the difference between a template and a film

Treat a moving graphic as a time-varying trajectory, not merely a cut.

- **Entry start:** sometimes sound begins with the actual object acceleration; sometimes a barely audible pre-lap helps anticipation. Use ear-led judgement, not a universal negative offset.
- **Peak velocity:** swish or noise density can peak near fastest visible motion.
- **Stop/settle:** tiny click or bodily settling sound *if the graphic implies contact/arrival*; don't give every title a slam.
- **Tail:** decay into room tone, avoiding sustained hiss under the next important word.
- **Duration:** compress/extend for actual easing. Softly crossfade between air textures rather than stretching a harsh transient to cover a slow pan.
- **Hit alignment:** percussive transients often feel earlier or later depending on animation, edit style and the sound's attack. A/B by frame; do not insist every attack lands exactly on cut frame.
- **Silence window:** a fraction of a beat without new effects can make the subsequent word sharper. No fixed duration works for all narration.

Avoid a pulse every 1.0 second simply because the video is an Instagram Reel. Human pacing follows thought groups and phrase structure.

## 5. Motion mapping for 2.5D parallax and camera travel

| Visual action | Sound logic | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Slow camera push into layered photo | Gently evolving environmental perspective or silent movement; possibly low diffuse air that blooms slightly | Wide, bright whoosh overpowering the shot |
| Foreground layer slides past | Light close material brush/air, directional if useful, proportional to velocity | Laser sci-fi streak, fake giant swoop |
| Background shifts sideways | Mostly ambient perspective continuity; no cue for every minor drift | Multiple overlapping whooshes |
| Rotating map/globe | Thin travelling air/texture; optional brief tactile cue on *important* location reveal | Comedic spinning sound |
| Nepal map pin lands | Single subdued location/marker click **only if motion has a landing point** | Cartoon pop + heavy boom |
| Paper/document reveals proof | Paper move + optional focus micro-accent; authentic texture | Detective crime-show door slam |
| Very fast whip transition | One clean movement layer, optional short low-mid punctuation | Full-length trailer rise and explosive hit |
| Data/graph animates | Continuity tone + one restrained accent on final number | Every bar gets a beep |
| Headlines enter over VO | Accent the semantic lead, not each text element | Five consecutive identical clicks |
| Quiet emotional image | Often only location air / room tone / spoken voice | Sad piano swells and panic heartbeat |

### 2.5D depth envelope exercise
For a 1.2-second parallax, write visual keyframes as A (entry), B (midpoint/maximum relative motion), C (settle). If sound warranted, choose a soft source whose onset is near A, energy follows A→B, then releases B→C. A separate micro-accent at C is optional. This is an *illustrative timing model*, not a prescribed audio file or exact placement.

## 6. Typography: sound only the meaning

For animated Nepali headlines, preserve written text and think in thought phrases rather than one SFX per letter. Prioritize comprehension, visual hierarchy, and speaker sync.

**Category A — informational:** One barely audible micro-click at a key label or final figure; most type enters silently.

**Category B — urgent warning:** A restrained dry air move with a single low-mid impact at the *central warning*, followed by an intentional reduction in effects so the audience hears the caution.

**Category C — intimate testimony:** Do not introduce aggressive effects on painful language. Small sounds from a real prop can support the scene; otherwise leave voice exposed.

**Category D — animated numbers:** One natural mechanical counting texture or fine ticking bed, then stop decisively at the final verified number. Avoid arcade slot-machine rolls and overly fast ticking that masks narration.

**Category E — call-to-action / helpline:** Silence or steady room tone while digits appear and are spoken. Never use rhythmic clicks between digits, as they impair recall.

For screen-only text without narration, subtle movement/focus sounds can be slightly more present, but silence is still an option.

## 7. Original sound signatures from everyday recordings

### A. “Documentary data reveal”
- Base: a single gentle paper-edge flick or tiny dry tactile click.
- Optional motion: a soft 100–300 ms air flutter *only when the visual travels quickly*.
- Optional weight: faint short low-mid round pulse, not a subwoofer slam.
- Edit: trim front, remove incidental breath/handling if not diegetic, shape a gentle release, roll off annoying brightness if it competes with consonants.
- Use: once for the key statistic. Make surrounding tiny numbers silent.

### B. “Credible scam message”
- Base: authentic quiet phone vibration or ordinary message alert; prefer one event.
- Bed: true room tone, very slight tension through environmental ambience rather than horror notes.
- Edit: stay low beneath narration; short notification, then return to baseline. Not every incoming visual message merits another SFX.
- Safety: don't represent an actual victim's phone unless real audio is provided/consented or the reenactment is clear.

### C. “2.5D side sweep”
- Base: naturally swishing cloth, paper, air or sourced movement.
- Transform: high-pass or EQ harsh top as needed; subtle pan following visual direction; manually follow velocity curve.
- Body: optional low-mid movement if object has perceived mass.
- Edit: align acceleration and deceleration; keep tail out of dialogue onset.
- Compare: try the movement with NO swoosh. Keep sound only if the spatial connection genuinely improves.

### D. “Airport migration scene”
- Bed: real terminal air/HVAC, distant and largely indistinct crowd, physically plausible reflections.
- Details: luggage wheels, single footsteps, distant boarding-area activity if supported by footage.
- Spatial: wide bed; close foreground source separated from diffuse far sound.
- Editorial: crossfade into the terminal before the cut if an establishing transition is desired; keep voices indistinct when no authentic language/location evidence.
- Avoid: fake announcements, recognizable third-party voices, generic “exotic” music cues.

### E. “Dignified warning / support message”
- Bed: very gentle room presence with an appropriate natural decay.
- Focus: the narrator speaking clearly; possibly no designed effects.
- Transition: fade out dramatic tension before help information so numbers and practical actions feel stable.
- Avoid: sobbing samples, alarm sirens, ominous heartbeats, violent stingers, miracle chimes.

### F. “Proof reveal in investigative sequence”
- Base: credible paper/book/key/pen Foley depending on the actual material.
- Tone: subtle shift in existing room or low texture can create intellectual gravity, without turning fact into melodrama.
- Punctuation: one small grounded attack at a decisive factual reveal, if warranted; avoid 808 “shock” hit.

## 8. The anti-template challenge

After drafting a 30-second cue map, pretend a junior editor dropped “whoosh + impact” on each transition. Now remove **at least half the proposed artificial punctuation candidates** and ask: can we make the video clearer with fewer, more authentic sounds? Choose one unique relationship between visual and sound for a hero moment. Do not enforce a literal 50% reduction for genres that genuinely require frequent action, but use the exercise to expose formulaic work.

A/B versions:
- **Cut A:** mostly truthful environments, tactile Foley, silence, micro-accents.
- **Cut B:** same sonic identity with slightly stronger attack/texture at genuine reveals.
Choose the version with better retained facts, less listener fatigue and appropriate emotion—not the one that feels bigger in solo playback.

## 9. Bad-to-good transformations

| Low-end novice result | Professional alternative |
|---|---|
| Each moving text block has the same whoosh | Assign one subtle scene-level air texture; accent only key statement |
| Every year/number change has retro digital beeps | Fine mechanical stepping with controlled fade, or silent animated roll |
| All important facts get bass booms | Reserve one understated low-mid accent for the central revelation |
| Trauma disclosure gets heartbeats and screams | Dry voice, believable room tone, empathic pause |
| Tech scam uses constant glitch distortion | Natural phone/UI Foley with gradual environmental tension |
| Ambient track abruptly disappears at a cut | Natural ambience L-cut/J-cut and crossfade |
| Wide whoosh plays across narrow mobile speaker | Keep the essential attack centered and mono-compatible |
| SFX solo sounds dramatic but masks VO | Judge at **final mix level** with narration, lower/reshape/omit |
| Sound pans opposite motion | Match spatial trajectory or keep centered if the image is non-diegetic |
| A library preview has words/musical content | Choose an isolated effect or a properly cleared recording; don't assume hidden content can be cut out |

## 10. Ethical documentary restraint

Sounds can become **fabricated claims**. Explicitly distinguish a dramatization from original recorded evidence. Avoid sensationalizing trauma, violence, deaths, border crossings, exploitation or other sensitive realities. There are times when an almost-empty soundtrack serves audience trust better than cinematic spectacle. Avoid stereotypes associating Nepal or another region with a single instrument, chaotic crowd, or danger drone. If the video is an NGO call for help, its primary purpose is credible, accessible understanding.
