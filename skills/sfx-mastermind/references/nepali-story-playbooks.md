# Nepali Documentary and NGO Story Playbooks

Use these methods for serious Nepali-language social videos, migration education, financial awareness, scams, mental-health support, government/civic information, and 9:16 film-like explainers with kinetic typography, 2.5D parallax, data and location animation.

**Editorial compass:** culturally grounded, dignified, clearly understandable, visually premium and sonically *understated*. Do not try to recreate another creator's trademark sound design. Make a distinctive coherent documentary identity. Respect exact user-provided Nepali text/numerals and VO timing.

## Universal Nepali audio rules

1. Read each whole Nepali sentence for its **thought groups**, not each on-screen word separately.
2. Identify the **hero idea** (often the statistic, factual risk, geographic move, response/action) and allocate a modest number of accents to it. Let grammatical particles and connective phrases breathe.
3. Protect pronunciation of Nepali consonants and numeric strings. Warn when music/SFX makes “४०”, “२४”, “१६६०…” or place names hard to hear.
4. Where all on-screen text must be Nepali, keep cue captions and timeline descriptions in Nepali if requested; search keywords may be English to improve retrieval.
5. Avoid stock regional “flute/tabla = Nepal” clichés unless actual music is requested and historically/culturally appropriate.
6. If voiceover is not recorded, no made-up second-level timing: use event-relative cues keyed to the exact spoken words and animation events.
7. For 2.5D, align air/texture dynamics to foreground/background speed and separation. Deep perspective does not always require an audible swish.
8. Enforce at least one quiet reset around difficult or high-value facts where appropriate.
9. For genuinely sensitive topics, do not add fictional cries, screaming, breathlessness, impacts, sirens or heartbeat as a way to create shock.

## Playbook A — Scam recruitment through social media

**Story:** Facebook/TikTok/WhatsApp posts promise extraordinary salaries, easy visas, jobs abroad; reveal hidden exploitation or financial theft.

**Sonic palette:** believable small phone vibration, restrained tap, thin realistic keyboard/touch sounds, indoor room tone, slight progressive environmental tension, single elegant pivot accent at reveal. Contrast hype vs skepticism without turning into cartoon comedy.

**Cue arc:**
1. Offer/ad appears — one **quiet authentic device** event; no glittery success chime.
2. Multiple ads flood screen — a light blended short texture or controlled single rhythmic motif, not 10 notifications.
3. Graphic currency figure dominates — optionally no cue, or one tiny low-mid pulse; keep VO amount clear.
4. Visual begins darkening/reality exposed — reduce device sounds, bring in believable room/space and a subtle tonal shift.
5. Warning/call for verification — strip down effects; dry, authoritative voice with a subtle room bed.

**Search starters:** `mobile vibration table muted real`, `one soft phone message alert clean realistic`, `finger swipe phone subtle close mic`, `keyboard typing slow few key presses`, `soft room tone office at night`, `restrained investigative subtle tension texture`, `short dry low-mid informational accent`.

**Reject:** “hacker” constant glitches, arcade pings, police siren without source, explosion on scam reveal, manipulative crying/victim audio.

## Playbook B — Cambodia / Thailand / Laos forced labor or trafficking awareness

**Story:** movement abroad, deceptively promising recruitment, high-risk working conditions. Treat uncertain statistics and circumstances as narrated claims, not audio-proven reality.

**Palette:** realistic departure hall/travel air, luggage wheel, short local room tone, computer/air-conditioner hum if depicted, sparse low-pressure sound design. Differentiate countries by *verified local sound provenance or graphics*, not stereotyped music/voices.

**Cue arc:** travel/map transition → one single lateral moving air cue at focal trip → crossfade into the actual depicted environment → gentle typing/room Foley only if visible → quiet pressure at risk disclosure → bare authoritative VO during help/action steps.

**Search:** `airport departure hall field recording distant crowd no intelligible speech`, `suitcase wheels smooth airport floor single pass`, `small office fluorescent hum quiet room tone`, `computer room fans distance ambiance`, `slow low pressure tension subtle not horror`.

**Truth safeguard:** a loud locked door, physical violence, screams, handcuffs, police radio, or other recognizable events may *falsely imply events shown actually happened*. Never invent them for drama.

## Playbook C — Nepali migrant departure / overseas employment

**Story:** dream, family uncertainty, airport departure, travel, hope and responsibility.

**Palette:** Kathmandu-specific field recording if verifiable, otherwise generic terminal clearly described as a design stand-in; real trolley, luggage handling, distant soft boarding-area wash, natural household room tone; perhaps restrained organic warmth near family if story asks for it.

**Key distinction:** emotional stakes are not the same as loud drama. Human credibility comes from **space, quiet and small object detail**. You do not need synthetic heartbreak sounds over a departure gate.

**2.5D scene:** when still travel photos move in depth, maintain the base airport/household space and apply one subtle close air/luggage layer only if the image implies source/action. Save a tonal shift for the factual pivot.

**Sound gap:** hold a small near-silent window before a strong factual line if rhythm allows. VO reenters with no theatrical slam.

## Playbook D — Animated statistics and infographics

**Typical visuals:** Nepal map/globe, climbing count/year transition, bars rising, deaths statistic, social-security fund participation, documents, pins, arrow travels.

**Sonic palette:** fine dry mechanical ticks, paper contact, controlled small low-mid pulses, soft air shifts, one accent at the *final number*. If multiple metrics appear sequentially, reserve strongest cue for the central point.

**Tactics:**
- Number count (e.g., २०२०→२०२६): continuous feather-light stepping or no sound; tiny settling marker on २०२६ if justified.
- A percentage ring increases: subtle motion air that follows acceleration, not repeated beeps.
- Graph bar reveals: no sound for each bar; a single grouping cue on completed data comparison.
- Map pin lands: soft tactile click/noise at actual land event; don't sound like a video game marker.
- A headline word turns red: sometimes silence gives the color contrast more force.
- Source footnote/document appears: quiet paper handling can feel tangible; source shouldn't be treated as a sensational shock reveal.

**Protect statistics:** keep SFX subdued during numerical VO to avoid mishearing critical figures.

## Playbook E — Fraudulent EU visa or travel offers

**Story:** seductive dream (“युरोप”), misleading certainty, warning about visit vs work visa, urgent precautions.

**Palette:** opening can be airy and understated; then believable phone notice, paper form rustle, light room tone, only modest tension shift. End on a clear, less processed warning/verification instruction.

**Search:** `quiet travel terminal air ambience`, `travel document paper shuffle`, `single phone notification short soft real`, `tactile stamp document subtle recorded` (only if actual paper/stamp visible), `restricted low pulse documentary`.

**Reject:** cash registers for every amount, dramatic visa stamp if no stamp exists in visual, alarm blaring under the line that explains the process.

## Playbook F — Mental-health support / suicide-prevention story

**Tone:** compassionate, dignified, factual, calm, supportive. Prefer voices, silence, authentic nonintrusive environment. Do not use shocking/horror effects.

**Emotional plan:**
1. Distress described — expose VO with subtle room air; no moaning/crying/heartbeat manipulation.
2. Consequences/facts — keep spoken information clean; allow brief pauses.
3. Support/family/professional help — gently open space using real-world, credible environmental warmth if picture supports it, **not** “magic healing” chimes.
4. Helpline/contact card — remove new effects; maybe silence or quiet stable bed. Digits should be heard/read comfortably and never be rushed by sound punctuation.

**Avoid:** cinematic panic crescendo, emergency sirens in generic stories, breathless panic samples, melodramatic instrumental flourishes, any cues that make help feel frightening.

## Playbook G — Government savings, banking or financial information

**Story:** earned funds, formal financial mechanism, interest, eligibility, what action to take.

**Palette:** paper application, restrained everyday bank/office room tone, quiet page turn, small geometric accent at key transition only, calm confidence.

**Do not:** use coin cascades, casino cash sounds, jackpot chimes or boastful luxury whooshes—especially if the video explains risk and savings rather than wealth promises.

**Search:** `soft document page turn clean close`, `pen signing paper close`, `office quiet room tone daytime`, `small neutral mechanical click understated`.

## Playbook H — NGO motion graphics with 2.5D typography, maps and parallax

**30-second sound dramaturgy example without fabricated timestamps:**

| Segment | Story purpose | Sound direction | Time source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening question | Curiosity, not shock | Dry voice + tiny environmental perspective, perhaps no punctuation | Lock to VO phrase opening |
| Location reveal | Ground geography | One soft parallax motion + map marker only if physically animated | Actual map animation event |
| Mechanism | Explain process | One coherent subtle tactile sound family, no event on every element | Relevant visual action |
| Key evidence | Attention to verified number | Reduce sound before figure; one short warm neutral accent only at key settlement | VO key fact + graphic end |
| Human implication | Respect emotion | Remove designed movement, preserve honest environment | Visual/VO emotional turn |
| Practical warning/help | Action clarity | Mostly voice and stable room tone | Entire critical instruction |
| End card | Retention and legibility | Silence/quiet fade, no “subscribe” pop unless requested | On final hold |

**Mandatory creative question:** “Would this still sound like a competent documentary with all the animations disabled?” If not, sound is masking weak editorial logic rather than supporting it.

## How to communicate to the editor

Use Nepali exact quoted cue phrase, English technical sound query if search performs better, and precise physical timing notes. Do not present a 20-second timeline when script speech speed is unknown. For each major cue provide a **quiet default** and a **punchier but credible backup**, including a clear option to omit it.
