# Global SFX Intelligence — International Discovery and Searchcraft

Use this reference when actively finding recorded sound effects, curating libraries, building sound-search terms or organizing assets.

## A. Think like a recording librarian, not a keyword spinner

A search result can fail along four axes: the **wrong source** (metal vs glass), **wrong action** (rattle vs impact), **wrong acoustic frame** (outdoor long reverberation when picture is close/macro), or **wrong editorial envelope** (3-second riser for a 0.2-second text movement). Search to isolate each dimension; swapping merely “cinematic” adjectives will not fix the error.

A high-recall search creates candidates; high-precision inspection eliminates them. For rare SFX use a **two-pass search**:
1. Recall pass: action, nouns, synonyms, UCS sound category, related materials.
2. Precision pass: isolated/room tone/no music, speed, microphone perspective, material, duration, location, format, variations, license.

Don't conflate search engines: some accept quotes or a minus prefix, others treat them as plain text. Test query semantics. In catalogs, use metadata facets if available: tags, pack, author, date, duration, channels, sample rate, file type, waveform, license.

### Example query transformation
Visual: “A document rises from a laptop in slow 2.5D parallax; the hero statistic appears while narrator speaks.”
- **Literal**: `paper sliding table quiet close foley`, `paper edge light flutter`.
- **Movement**: `short airy passing left to right subtle`, `gentle fabric swish isolated`.
- **Focus**: `soft low-mid pulse dry controlled decay`.
- **Perspective**: `paper close up foley room tone isolated`.
- **Avoid**: loud sword swoosh, laser startup, horror stinger, 808 bass drop, repeated mouse clicks.
- **Test**: One tiny paper flick at document acceleration + 0–1 warm micro-pulse on the largest fact; possibly no effect beneath narration.

## C. Search the **right lane** for the right physical problem

- **Everyday concrete one-shots**: professional library, clean Foley vendor, Freesound CC0 if quick and original creator verified.
- **Specific global cities and regions**: actual field recordists / documentary locations, indexed sound libraries with provenance; verify recording location rather than guessing from filename.
- **Complex cinematic transitions**: specialist sound design collections, then redesign with restrained physical sounds.
- **Sensitive documentary**: natural location tone and true Foley, not invented cries, sirens or gunshots.
- **Nature / rare animals**: wildlife specialist, scientifically identified archive, properly licensed recordist file.
- **Vintage machinery/aircraft/cars**: specialist field recording packs with proper gear and perspective.
- **Nepali/South Asian setting**: authentic local field recordings when available; otherwise explicitly mark a geographically generic environment rather than claiming it was recorded in Nepal.
- **UI / tech**: capture your own legally allowed device sounds, use carefully designed neutral tones, search `minimal interface tactile click` instead of `futuristic hacker glitch`.

## D. Four rounds of efficient global search

**Round 1 — 90-second broad conceptual pass:** 2–3 candidate sounds from two source lanes. Search the literal material and action, then the cinematic category. Identify vocabulary mismatch.

**Round 2 — authenticity and shape:** Add distance, space, duration and exclusions. Search a specialist or regional archive if the effect must convincingly come from a real place/object. Seek 2–4 meaningful alternatives, not clones.

**Round 3 — editability and rights:** Inspect originals, sample length, clipped/noisy heads, hidden speech/music, unwanted reverberation, repetition, possible loop and valid license for client/NGO use.

**Round 4 — audition in picture:** If media access exists, A/B contenders **at expected mix volume beneath voice**, not solo and loud. Evaluate whether it distracts at mobile volume. Shortlist one + fallback; stop when further exploration has low expected improvement.

For simple requests, one or two rounds may suffice. For unusual culturally specific sounds, use deeper searches; report searches actually completed.

## E. Ontology and tagging

A robust record includes category, subcategory, source, action, material, intensity, recording distance, acoustic space, timing, duration, stereo width, format, source/owner, license, edit points, negative characteristics, emotional weight, and version. Use Universal Category System (UCS) as a reference vocabulary (https://universalcategorysystem.com/), but do not invent formal UCS IDs or claim an asset is UCS-tagged unless verified.

| Source family | Search family words | Distinguish by |
|---|---|---|
| Air/transition | whoosh, swish, rush, air displacement, pass-by, whip | short/long, natural/synthetic, direction, tail, frequency |
| Hits | knock, thud, impact, thump, clack, hit, slams, accents | material, mass, attack, ring, chamber |
| Paper/textiles | rustle, crinkle, flick, flutter, swipe, cloth, fabric | distance, stiffness, speed, weight |
| Tech/office | keys, UI blip, vibration, device chime, fan, electronic hum | analogue vs digital, tiny vs intrusive, no voices |
| Transport | airport concourse, luggage roll, cabin hum, engine, bus stop, footsteps | recording authenticity, location, motion |
| Location | field recording, room tone, crowd walla, street, air, traffic, wind | people intensity, perspective, clean or intelligible speech |
| Tension | low drone, evolving air, tonal pressure, subtle pulse, distant rumble | pitches vs dissonance, speech masking, emotional ethics |
| Relief | warm environmental opening, softer high-air, soft organic presence | tasteful, non-sentimental, avoid stock “hope chimes” |

## F. Advanced discovery tools and metadata

- Soundly supports precise search operators such as `-term`, quoted phrases and named libraries (`library: terms`) under its documented semantics; read the current support guide rather than assuming all operators are portable: https://getsoundly.com/faq/are-there-any-advanced-search-options/ .
- Pro Sound Effects SoundQ offers local/cloud search, UCS-optimized metadata, Boolean queries, keyword translation and editor workflow; exact functions and rights depend on installed version and license: https://www.prosoundeffects.com/soundq .
- Freesound has an API for search/metadata/content similarity, but use it only when authenticated and permitted; API access terms and individual file CC licenses are separate: https://freesound.org/docs/api/ .
- For multi-channel ambiences, preview channels separately if possible; don't reduce to mono by discarding useful surround information without listening.
- Organize local files by `scene/cueID_source_action_perspective_take_variant` and store original basename + URI/license evidence separately. Do not rename away the vendor's traceable source ID.

## G. Actual multilingual search methodology

Translate **source and action**, not generic marketing phrases. Keep English technical words like `foley`, `stereo`, `WAV`, `field recording` if likely recognized by the catalog. Use language of the *recordist/community* when relevant; language selection is for discovery, not evidence of where the sound was made.

| Search intent | Useful query variants (examples) |
|---|---|
| Airport departure | `airport terminal crowd ambience`, `एयरपोर्ट प्रस्थान हल आवाज`, `हवाई अड्डा टर्मिनल वातावरण` |
| Rain on tin roof | `rain tin roof close`, `जस्ताको छानामा पानी परेको आवाज`, `lluvia techo metal sonido` |
| Moving luggage wheel | `luggage suitcase wheels tile`, `maleta ruedas suelo aeropuerto`, `valise roulette aeroport son` |
| Shuffling paper | `paper rustle close foley`, `पाना पल्टाउने आवाज`, `papier froissement bruitage` |
| Busy market | `market outdoor ambience traffic`, `बजारको चहलपहल आवाज`, `mercado ambiente calle grabación` |
| Distant public announcement | `airport distant PA indistinct no speech`, `aéroport annonce brouhaha ambiance` |

For Nepali work: translate scene vocabulary accurately and preserve user-provided Devanagari script in cue sheets. If English gives better results for sound effects, use English search terms while keeping on-screen narrative and explanation in Nepali when requested. For any geography-specific field recording, verify source notes, not nationality inferred from accent or tags.

## I. Stop criteria and output honesty

Stop searching when a technically usable, emotionally fitting, licensable primary and reasonable alternative exist. Do not keep scrolling because the video can accept 20 clips. In final recommendations, distinguish: exact asset link vs category search, fee required vs freely available, preview actually audited vs merely indexed, license checked vs unknown, actual geographic provenance vs visual-appropriate generic substitute.
