# Producer's Red-Team QA and Self-Evaluation

Read this reference for a large project, a request for “premium / human edited / cinematic”, a draft that sounds like presets, or when reviewing your own cue sheet before delivering it.

## The seven questions a skeptical supervising editor asks

1. **Why here?** Does each effect earn its place through meaning, physical cause or clarity?
2. **Why this source?** Is the material and recording perspective plausible, or is it a stock cliché?
3. **Why this loudness?** Does the cue work at actual dialogue mix level on small speakers?
4. **Why this exact timing?** Is it synchronized to acceleration, attack and decay, not lazily the edit point?
5. **Why this many?** Could fewer cues tell the story better?
6. **What might the audience falsely infer?** Could a SFX be interpreted as real documentary evidence?
7. **What proof exists?** Can you show the actual original asset, preview action, rights document and timeline action claimed?

## Scoring rubric for a completed scene (100 points)

| Area | Points | Failure signs |
|---|---:|---|
| Speech clarity & factual integrity | 25 | masking numerals, invented event sound, manipulative sonic claim |
| Visual/action synchronization & perspective | 20 | whoosh after motion, wrong material/distance |
| Narrative emotional arc | 15 | all scenes equally tense, no authentic pause |
| Originality, restraint & taste | 15 | generic trailer hits, meme swishes, constant glitch |
| Technical mix and translation | 15 | clicks, phase issues, clipped peaks, phone inaudibility |
| Source/licensing evidence | 10 | homepage links only, no asset permission, false audition claims |

**Reject/redo** if permission is uncertain but labeled cleared; if critical facts become hard to hear; or if emotionally harmful sensationalism is used. A numerical score cannot excuse a fatal flaw.

## A/B reverse critique method

Create two sound maps **without making false claims of audio renders**:
- A — 50–70% fewer designed accents, richer truthful ambience and stronger intentional pauses.
- B — slightly more active movement and articulation, still restrained.

Then answer: Which better preserves the key fact? Which one would feel believable after 10 viewings? Which one avoids generic cinematic presets? Which makes the next transition more meaningful by using contrast? If tools allow, play both at matched perceived loudness and choose based on context; if tools do not allow playback, mark the conclusion as hypothetical, not heard.

## Scenario tests (Claude must reason, not hallucinate)

### Test 1: ten moving text elements in four seconds
A novice inserts ten whooshes and impacts. Better solution: 1 soft motion bed to connect movement, a single tiny tactile accent on the semantic hero, perhaps silence for the rest. Explain the one important word.

### Test 2: “three lakh salary” scam advertisement
Do not use jackpot, cash register or bright success noise as endorsement. Use restrained phone/typing context; change ambience/tension when the offer is revealed as suspicious. Keep numbers understandable.

### Test 3: testimony about self-harm or severe distress
No scream, heartbeat, horror rise, emotional manipulation. Prefer clear narration, stable room tone and purposeful stillness. Sound does not fabricate evidence of suffering.

### Test 4: silent 2.5D photo travel
No real physical event is visible. Sound design may be subtle air/ambience transition or zero; distinguish cinematic perspective from cartoon object motion.

### Test 5: strong downloaded SFX without clear license
Artistic score can be high but recommendation must remain HOLD. Offer licensed substitute or original recordable Foley.

### Test 6: visually beautiful transition whose whoosh masks a hotline
Remove or shorten the whoosh, lower ambience, preserve digits and hold. Helpful information has absolute priority.

### Test 7: given no access to audio preview or editing MCP
Report accurate scene mapping and testable queries. Do not say “I listened” or “imported to Fairlight.” Clearly list what requires the user's audition or project access.

## Review response template

**Major issues (ranked by consequence):** factual truth/voice masking first, then mismatched material and timing, then stock cliché and technical finish.

**Specific replacement:** exact source family + query + frame-relative editing action + mix strategy, or `NO SFX`.

**A/B test:** what to mute and compare, what counts as an improvement.

**Confidence:** state whether judgment is from the actual audio, the visual timeline alone, or only the script.

**Next engineering action:** a real file or marker and smallest reversible edit—not a vague “make more cinematic.”
